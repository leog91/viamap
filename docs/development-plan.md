# Development plan

## Architecture

- `src/routes/` and `src/router.tsx`: a single TanStack Start page, statically styled and rendered by the Worker before JavaScript loads.
- `src/features/explorer/`: React controls own origin and radius; a browser-only MapLibre component owns camera, event handlers, and map layers. One GeoJSON source contains the circle and origin point. Updating it does not recreate the map.
- `src/geo/`: pure Turf functions in metres, using longitude–latitude coordinate order. `src/config/` holds the initial Dublin view, not a geographic restriction or a private address.
- Phase 2 adds `scripts/gtfs/`, transport models, and versioned files under `public/data/`. Keep raw archives and full schedules outside `public/`.

No server functions, database, accounts, query cache, routing engine, or separate API for Phase 1. Use the Cloudflare Vite plugin for the `ssr` environment and Wrangler's Start server entry. This follows the [TanStack setup guide](https://tanstack.com/start/latest/docs/framework/react/build-from-scratch) and [Cloudflare's framework guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/framework-guides/web-apps/tanstack-start/).

## Milestones

## Requested city expansion: Amsterdam and Quilmes

- **Amsterdam:** public default origin is Europaplein metro station. The Nov 17, 2026 [OVapi snapshot](https://gtfs.ovapi.nl/nl/NL-20261117.gtfs.zip) was filtered to operator `GVB` before trip expansion, then to the Amsterdam boarding study area. It yields 1,237 stops and 73 route IDs, with calendar dates November 16, 2026–January 31, 2027. Within the initial 500 m there are 11 boarding points and seven services, including metro 52 and tram 4. This scope intentionally excludes NS and other regional operators. `licenses.txt` lists CC0 for general data and ODbL for shapes; ticketing data was not imported. The adapted shapes and their [notices](../public/data/amsterdam/NOTICE.txt) are publicly accessible.
- **Quilmes:** the [Buenos Aires bus GTFS](https://cdn.buenosaires.gob.ar/datosabiertos/datasets/transporte-y-obras-publicas/colectivos-gtfs/colectivos-gtfs.zip) lists service dates ending December 31, 2019, and the [rail GTFS](https://cdn.buenosaires.gob.ar/datosabiertos/datasets/transporte-y-obras-publicas/trenes-gtfs/trenes-gtfs.zip) includes Quilmes but its service calendar ends April 30, 2020. The national [AMBA bus route dataset](https://datos.transporte.gob.ar/dataset/recorridos-de-servicios-de-colectivos-amba) is a KML of trajectories, not current boardable stops and schedules. The selector therefore opens a map centred on Quilmes station and links official Línea Roca travel information without loading or inventing a schedule dataset.

The nested official Subte GTFS archive's regular calendars end December 31, 2021. Location views have separate static files and UI so they do not imply scheduled or boardable service.
- A visible city notice distinguishes limited operator coverage, location-only data, unavailable schedules and schedule snapshots whose dates don't cover today.
- **Argentina network reference follow-up:** CABA now also renders SBASE’s six Subte alignments (A–E and H). Quilmes renders 2020 archived alignments for the Roca branches to La Plata and Bosques via Quilmes, cross-checked against the official current branch publications. Reference layers are independent of the chosen origin, cannot imply service frequency or reachability, have their own map toggle and do not participate in journey calculations.

- **Cross-city navigation:** selecting a point inside another supported area switches datasets without changing camera or radius. Panning offers a city suggestion at a useful zoom rather than changing the selected origin. These recognition zones are not coverage boundaries.
- **Barcelona reference overlay:** 13 FGC routes use the agency’s published geometry (CC BY 4.0), without publishing schedule claims or conflating this subset with the full TMB, Rodalies, tram and bus networks.
- **Requested follow-up:** place search, two-origin comparisons, an opt-in Valhalla walking overlay and selected-stop walk measurement, and scheduled direct trips are implemented. Direct trips are browser calculations over compressed per-route schedules, not a transfer or door-to-door engine. Amsterdam's national feed now includes domestic NS; Barcelona's official Renfe/Rodalies feed now adds scheduled data separately from its FGC reference geometry. TMB, wider AMBA bus schedules and current machine-readable Quilmes/Subte schedules remain unavailable or unverified for this app.

Keep a keyboard alternative to pointer selection. Accept when the map selection and radius controls work locally, geographic tests pass, and a Workers build can be previewed and packaged.

### Phase 2 implementation and handoff

Read against the [GTFS Schedule reference](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/reference/). Namespace IDs per source; validate references and coordinate ranges, preserve stop order and repeated stops, optional direction, shape sequence, pickup restrictions, service calendars/exceptions, and integer seconds since service-day start (including `25:xx:xx`). Choose trips serving eligible local stops but retain full ordered trip patterns and downstream shapes beyond the study bounds. Distinguish drop-off-only stops from boarding stops. Group only equivalent patterns (route, direction, shape, ordered stops and permissions), keeping schedule associations offline.

`scripts/gtfs/import.ts` streams CSV with an offline two-pass stop-time scan, then serializes full retained trip patterns and schedules as gzip JSON under `.gtfs/dublin/`. Pattern deduplication retains direction, shape, stop sequence, heads and boarding permissions; times and service calendars stay offline. The index under `public/data/dublin/` holds provenance links, actual earliest/latest calendar dates (not a promise of daily service), coverage, and stop-to-route relationships. Shared NTA platforms are merged only on identical upstream ID and coordinates; namespaced platform IDs remain in the index for linking to trip calls. A stop counts as boardable only if an allowed pickup can be followed by an allowed drop-off on an active trip; pickup/drop-off types 2 and 3 may require advance arrangements. The importer currently rejects GTFS frequency-based feeds rather than silently assuming those trips are fixed-time.

The bundled Nov 18 snapshot has **4,891 boarding points, 188 route IDs, and 1,766 trip patterns** across the four feeds. The public index is about **1.14 MB raw**. The initial 500 m Marino circle has **21 stops and 17 services**, including DART at Clontarf Road and both Dublin Bus and Go-Ahead at Crescent Place. Use a single browser load and a linear distance scan until profiling shows a need to partition or index the data.

### Phase 3 implementation and limitations

- The importer accumulates metres on the original GTFS shape using Turf. Trip calls are matched in order, using GTFS shape-distance hints when present and consistent with coordinates. Otherwise, offline matching considers multiple candidate positions to distinguish loops and nearby parallel path segments. The importer rejects alignments requiring a snap beyond 85 m or an ambiguous choice between materially separated passes. This is a conservative heuristic, not a guarantee of upstream geometry quality.
- Route assets deduplicate identical display patterns on the same shape, export eligible platform/offsets, and simplify shapes to an approximately 4 m geometric tolerance while retaining measures from the original polyline. Coordinates and cumulative centimetres are delta-encoded. Assets are per route and content-addressed; the browser only fetches enabled nearby routes, reuses them as filters change, and batches updates. The snapshot has geometry for **165 of 188 routes**. It omitted **196 of 1,766 input patterns** before display deduplication. Geometry assets total about **4.15 MB raw**; the initial Marino search uses 17 assets totaling **637 KB raw**, plus the stop index.
- A directional line starts at the eligible boarding offset and ends at the last allowed downstream alighting stop. A further eligible boarding offset resets the distance, so the colour represents the shortest onboard distance since a nearby boarding on that pattern. The map keeps both directions and variants where available. Overlapping coloured paths are not a timetable, a measure of overall journey cost, or evidence of intermediate alighting.
- The map uses one GeoJSON source and MapLibre data-driven line colours for 250 m segments, capping the ramp at 12 km. [MapLibre `line-gradient`](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/layers/#line-gradient) uses normalized line progress and does not support per-feature ramp stops. Fixed-distance segments avoid stretching the scale for short journeys; colour changes occur in small steps. Bus, tram, rail and per-route filters affect the map only. Scheduled travel times, walking, waiting, transfers and isochrones remain out of scope.
